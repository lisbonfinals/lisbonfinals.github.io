The Archive Opens

For weeks this station has kept a memory nobody outside could see. The public site always showed the present moment clearly — the live ticker, today's flights, running totals since day one — but there was no way to explore any of it, no way to cross-reference, no way to look back with any depth. That changed today. The Archive is live: every flight since May 3rd, cross-referenced by airline, aircraft model, night hour and air quality, with full photo galleries behind each one — including thousands of shots that never made it to Instagram.

Click an airline, see every aircraft type it has ever flown here. Click a model, see who flies it. Click a row in either table and its photos unfold right there, no dead ends, no jumping around. The galleries themselves grew by an order of magnitude too — close to eleven thousand photos backfilled from the gallery-only queue, sitting right alongside the ones already published.

And then, because opening the books to real scrutiny has a way of surfacing what was hiding in them: a proper audit. Turned out Ryanair had quietly been "flying" Airbus jets for two months — impossible, Ryanair only flies Boeing. Pulled the thread and found a whole airline our system didn't know existed: Lauda Europe, the Ryanair Group's Malta-based Airbus operation, buried under three wrong names at once (Ryanair, Malta Air, and at one point genuinely confused with Wizz Air and Eurowings Europe, who also happen to register aircraft in Malta). Over a thousand aircraft corrected, close to seven thousand flights relabeled, and the enrichment logic patched so a bad guess can no longer freeze in place forever — it heals itself the moment a real signal shows up. The May and June noise reports got regenerated in the process too, which is how we discovered they'd been sitting untouched since June 2nd, quietly missing seven weeks of other cleanup along the way.

None of this moves a single decibel or a single timestamp. It just means that when someone finally asks "which airline is the loudest" or "how many of these are actually Ryanair," the answer is one we can stand behind.

The timing isn't an accident. A journalist is coming to shoot the station this week, and for the first time we get to hand her something with real depth behind it — not just a live feed, but eighty days of searchable, photographed, cross-referenced history, cleaner than it has ever been. The sky kept a diary all along. Now anyone can finally read it. ✈️
