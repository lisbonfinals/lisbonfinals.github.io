Atmospheric conditions

The wind
decides everything.

LPPT's runway runs SSW–NNE. The wind decides which way traffic flows. With a north wind, aircraft approach from the south — descending over the Tejo and the city centre before touching down on RWY 03, crossing directly over my terrace on final approach. With a south wind, they take off on RWY 21, climbing towards the SSW, crossing the terrace before reaching the Tejo.

RWY 03
North wind — landing over the terrace ✦
Planes approach from the south and land over my terrace. Close, slow, full-frame — most impactful shots.
RWY 21
South wind — takeoff over the terrace
Planes take off and climb over my terrace heading north. Higher, faster — less impactful shots.

Latest captures
Livefrom the terrace

Each detected aircraft triggers the full pipeline. A YOLO-trained model evaluates every frame and scores the photo from 1 to 5 — only the best make it to Instagram, without any human input. Two sensors capture what the eye can't see: an ADS1115 circuit measures the peak decibel level as the aircraft passes, and an MPU-6050 accelerometer records the vibration pulse transmitted through the building structure. One plane. Three data points.

Outdoor monitoring

Air quality
at the terrace.

Every aircraft that passes over this terrace burns Jet A-1 fuel — releasing ultrafine particles directly into the approach corridor. The PMS5003 laser particle counter measures PM1.0, PM2.5 and PM10 in real time, correlating each reading with the passing flight. A concrete answer to a question nobody else is asking here.
